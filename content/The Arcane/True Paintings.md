True Paintings are the purest expression of will a being is capable of. It is a mastery of self and intent. Very few has ever achieved this feat of existence, so little is publicly known about the standard properties of a True Painting. Often it is a physical/metaphysical manifestation of a being's will and intent. If such a being desires the death of something in front of them, manifesting their True Painting, the way they see the world would "paint" itself on the surrounding world, twisting to create situations to bring the death of that intended individual. True Paintings are **your** world, the way you live it.

There are a few documented examples of True Paintings being manifested.
[[Queen Ylindara]] of the [[Trukalian Empire]]- Taken directly from Imperial Scribing of a Royal Hearing:
"1273 PSA, 1 Ver.
Multaq of Burun stands before the Royal Gaze to be judged for the crime of lèse-majesté.
Notable excerpts from trial as follows:
Multaq states: 'It is the commandment of the royal subject to recount history under aristocracy. For the moment a people forget to remember and question, is the time where resistance fails to begin.'
Marquis Orun replies: 'Mockery of the Queen's loss of her lover and her sister, merely days after their assassination is not a recounting of history, but a defiling of grief. To re-enact such a scene, blaming such on personal woes, is telling of character. And what say of the use of enchantment on your crowds?'
Multaq barely utters words of response before the Queen herself commands the world. **Death.** 
And I must say, forgive the humble scribe for his poor vocabulary, for all of my studying did not prepare me to recount this happening.
The royal chamber distorts as the walls discolor to display transmogrified chessboards and other board gaming concepts. The gilded pillars that aline the walls of this hall turned malignant. The air itself turned heavy and pushed us all to the floor, kneeling. The sunlit windows transitioned to a miasmic red, as if blood covered the windows. The... soliders that once were the pillars adorning this hall turned to Multaq, the center of ire, raised the massive sword it weilded, and severed the man from our mortal coil. 
And thus the room quickly reverted to close to how it was, adding only a new tension and fear across the room, and a bloody stain where the defendant once stood."
