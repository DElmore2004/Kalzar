I was born a century ago in [[the Shards of Eorza]], a land where magic runs as freely as water and knowledge is treasured above all else. My family stood among the most respected, wielders of both magical influence and arcane mastery, and they raised me with warmth and expectation in equal measure. From the moment I could speak, I was surrounded by tomes, scrolls, and spells, learning not only the intricacies of magic but also its philosophy and place in the world. Yet, for all their wisdom, the Shards were a society of hoarders, our knowledge was kept tightly guarded, a privilege of the few rather than a gift for the many. Even as a child, I believed this to be wrong. What is knowledge if it cannot be shared? What is magic if it cannot inspire? It was this belief that set me apart. In time, it set me against those I loved most. My mentor, who was also my confidant and my lover, taught me how to harness my wild talent, shaping chaos into brilliance. But when I spoke of taking our teachings beyond the Shards, of giving the wider world the gift of understanding magic, he turned against me. He called the people of this continent lesser, unworthy of the truths I cherished. His rejection cut deeper than any wound, but it only steeled my resolve. With no allies left, I left my homeland and crossed the seas alone, carrying nothing but my faith in magic and the dream of a world where it could be celebrated openly.

That was thirty years ago. In those early days, I found welcome in a small temple and quickly rose to the position of High Priestess of [[Aleria, Guardian of the Fold]]. I guided acolytes, healed the weary, and taught those brave enough to learn, even as whispers of fear spread through the land. When the war began, fear hardened into hatred. Magic became a scapegoat, a curse in the eyes of the people I had once sworn to protect. They burned the temple, tore down its stones, and scattered my students to the wind. I led those I could to safety, but in the end we parted ways, each seeking survival in our own fashion. The temple lies in ruins, a husk of what we built together, and I walk the world alone once more. Now, I am weary. My talent remains strong, but I dare not flaunt it; in these lands, magic is as dangerous to its wielder as it is to their enemies. I speak only of seeking a new home, never of the fact that I am, in truth, a refugee from a war that despised what I hold sacred. My deepest fear, one I bury beneath ritual and rhetoric, is that this continent will never change, that magic will never find a safe place here. If that day comes, I may return to the Shards in defeat, proving right every voice that told me my dream was folly. Until then, I press forward, clinging to hope even as it flickers like a dying flame.
